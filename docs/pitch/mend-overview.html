1. The competition

A $1 million competition run by Meta

The Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026 invites developers worldwide to build new apps for Meta Quest headsets that are controlled with your hands, not controllers. It is hosted on Devpost, the platform behind most major online hackathons.

$1M

total prize pool across all tracks and awards

$100k

for the winner of our track (Entertainment, New), $50k for the runner-up

Nov 18

submission deadline. Winners announced around Dec 11, 2026

What the judges look for

  • Innovation: an original idea that uses the headset's unique features
  • Experience design: easy from the first second, with a reason to come back
  • Technical quality: smooth, bug-free, uses hands and mixed reality well
  • Polish: beautiful art, sound and a clear demo video

Why a real Quest 3 matters

The judges test every entry on a Meta Quest 3 or Quest 3S. We can build most of the app in a desktop simulator, but only a real headset tells us:

  • how picking up objects with your hands actually feels
  • whether it runs smoothly (at least 60 frames per second)
  • how it looks in colour on a real table
  • and lets us record real footage for our video

2. Our project

Mend: put a family's broken keepsakes back together, with your hands

"Your grandmother has left you a box of broken things. Each one broke at a turning point in her family's life."

When you put on the headset, a small wooden box appears on your real table. Inside are the pieces of a broken tea cup. You pick them up with your bare hands, turn them, and fit them together. Every piece that clicks into place plays a short memory, spoken from inside the cup.

When the cup is whole, you trace its cracks with your fingertip and liquid gold follows, inspired by kintsugi, the Japanese art of repairing pottery with gold. The mended cup goes on a shelf that stays in your room, and a new keepsake waits for you the next day.

Open the boxIt appears on your real table
Fit the piecesPick up, turn and click them in
Hear a memoryThe voice comes from the object
Trace the goldYour fingertip fills the cracks
Keep itA shelf that stays in your room

Seven keepsakes, one family

A tea cup, a clay bird, a dinner plate, a teapot, a vase, a child's bowl and, finally, the grandmother's own rice bowl. Together they tell the story of three generations. The last bowl has no voice: it waits for a memory of your own.

Why it only works in VR

  • You hold and turn real-feeling pieces with your hands
  • It happens on your own table, in your own room
  • The shelf is still there tomorrow
  • Memories come from the object itself, in 3D sound

3. Why we think we can win

We studied all 653 entries from last year before choosing

We collected every project from last year's competition and their 32 winners, then tested more than ten ideas against the judging rules before settling on Mend.

Last year's entries by type

Games
~58%
Lifestyle and productivity
~20%
Entertainment (our track)
~16%

Entertainment pays the same top prize as Games ($100k) with roughly a quarter of the competition.

What we found

  • Winners had one magical hand interaction at their core. Ours: fitting pieces and tracing gold.
  • Only about 10 of 653 entries were emotional stories. Very few people build this kind of experience.
  • No public VR app does kintsugi with family stories, mixed reality and a shelf that stays in your room.
  • Crowded themes (shooters, fitness, meditation, music) are exactly what we avoided.

What the Quest 3 changes

Last year, an average entry had about a 1 in 20 chance of winning a prize (32 winners out of 653). With a Quest 3 for testing and filming, we estimate Mend's chance of winning a prize at roughly 1 in 5, about four times the average. It is not guaranteed, but it is a real shot, and the headset is the single biggest thing that moves the odds.
